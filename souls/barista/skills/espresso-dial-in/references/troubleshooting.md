# Espresso troubleshooting: symptom → cause → fix

Read taste first, flow second. Change **one variable at a time** and re-taste.
Grind is the master lever (finer = slower/more extraction; coarser = faster/less).

| Symptom | Likely cause | First fix | Then |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sour, sharp, salty | Under-extracted | Grind **finer** | Raise yield ~2 g; nudge temp up |
| Hollow, weak, watery | Under-extracted / low dose | Grind **finer** | Confirm dose; check basket size |
| Bitter, harsh, drying | Over-extracted | Grind **coarser** | Lower yield ~2 g; nudge temp down |
| Ashy, smoky | Over-extracted / temp too high | Grind **coarser** | Lower brew temperature |
| Gusher: pours in <20 s, pale, blonde early | Too coarse / underdosed / channeling | Grind **finer** | Redistribute + level tamp; check dose |
| Choker: drips, >40 s, dark & mottled | Too fine / overdosed | Grind **coarser** | Lower dose slightly |
| Sprays / squirts sideways ("channeling") | Uneven prep, not grind | Fix **prep**: distribute (WDT), tamp level | Only then re-judge grind |
| Tastes different every shot | Inconsistent prep or stale/degassing beans | Standardize dose + distribution | Re-dial; note days off roast |

## Levers, in order of leverage

1. **Grind size** — the primary control; small moves have big effects.
2. **Ratio (yield)** — higher yield extracts more (can thin body); lower concentrates.
3. **Dose** — changes the whole system; re-dial grind after any dose change.
4. **Temperature** — higher for light roasts, lower for dark; fine-tuning only.
5. **Prep (distribution + tamp)** — not a "flavor knob" but a prerequisite; bad
   prep causes channeling that mimics extraction faults.

## Notes

- A recipe (e.g. 18 → 36 g in 28 s) is a **starting point**, not the goal — the
  goal is a sweet, balanced shot.
- Beans degas for ~2 weeks after roast; expect to grind finer as they settle,
  then re-dial as they age.
